1. Consider the following statements: 1. Fiscal deficit equals total expenditure minus total receipts excluding borrowings. 2. Revenue deficit equals revenue expenditure minus revenue receipts. 3. Primary deficit equals fiscal deficit plus interest payments. Which of the statements given above is/are correct?

  1. 1 only
  2. 2 only
  3. 1 and 2 only
  4. 1, 2 and 3

Answer: C) 1 and 2 only

Statement 1 is correct: the fiscal deficit shows the government's total borrowing requirement. Statement 2 is correct: it measures the shortfall of revenue receipts against revenue expenditure. Statement 3 is wrong: primary deficit is fiscal deficit minus interest payments, showing borrowing needed for non-interest spending. So 1 and 2 only.

2. Consider the following statements: 1. The Cash Reserve Ratio is maintained by banks in the form of gold and government securities. 2. The Statutory Liquidity Ratio can be maintained in cash, gold and approved securities. 3. The RBI does not pay interest on the CRR balances kept by banks. Which of the statements given above is/are correct?

  1. 1 and 2 only
  2. 2 and 3 only
  3. 1 and 3 only
  4. 3 only

Answer: B) 2 and 3 only

Statement 1 is wrong: CRR must be held as cash balance with the RBI, not as gold or securities. Statement 2 is correct: SLR is held by banks themselves in liquid assets such as cash, gold and approved securities. Statement 3 is correct: no interest is paid on CRR balances. So 2 and 3 only.

3. Consider the following statements: 1. The repo rate is the rate at which the RBI lends short-term funds to banks against government securities. 2. The reverse repo rate is the rate at which banks borrow from the RBI. 3. The Marginal Standing Facility rate is set above the repo rate. Which of the statements given above is/are correct?

  1. 1 and 3 only
  2. 1 and 2 only
  3. 2 and 3 only
  4. 1, 2 and 3

Answer: A) 1 and 3 only

Statement 1 is correct: repo is collateralised lending by RBI. Statement 2 is wrong: under reverse repo the RBI borrows from banks, absorbing liquidity. Statement 3 is correct: the MSF rate sits 25 basis points above the repo rate and forms the upper limit of the policy corridor. So 1 and 3 only.

4. Which one of the following is NOT a function of the Reserve Bank of India?

  1. Acting as banker to the Government
  2. Acting as lender of last resort to banks
  3. Custodian of the country's foreign exchange reserves
  4. Issuing one-rupee currency notes

Answer: D) Issuing one-rupee currency notes

The RBI manages government accounts, supports banks as the lender of last resort and holds the foreign exchange reserves. However, one-rupee notes and all coins are issued by the Government of India through the Ministry of Finance, and the RBI only circulates them. The RBI issues notes of Rs 2 and above. So the last option is not an RBI function.

5. Consider the following statements: 1. The money multiplier is inversely related to the reserve ratio such as CRR. 2. M1 includes time deposits held with banks. 3. M3 equals M1 plus time deposits with banks. Which of the statements given above is/are correct?

  1. 1 only
  2. 1 and 2 only
  3. 2 and 3 only
  4. 1 and 3 only

Answer: D) 1 and 3 only

Statement 1 is correct: a higher reserve ratio cuts the lending a deposit can support, lowering the multiplier. Statement 2 is wrong: M1 is currency with the public, demand deposits and other RBI deposits; time deposits enter in M3. Statement 3 is correct: M3 is the broad money measure. So 1 and 3 only.

8. Assertion (A): The Monetary Policy Committee determines the policy repo rate in India. Reason (R): The Monetary Policy Committee has six members, three from the RBI and three appointed by the Central Government. Choose the correct option:

  1. Both A and R are true but R is not the correct explanation of A
  2. Both A and R are true and R is the correct explanation of A
  3. A is true but R is false
  4. A is false but R is true

Answer: B) Both A and R are true and R is the correct explanation of A

The Assertion is true: since 2016 the statutory six-member MPC decides the policy rate to meet the inflation target. The Reason is also true: the Governor, a Deputy Governor and one RBI officer are joined by three external members named by the Government. But the composition does not explain why the MPC fixes the rate; that is its statutory mandate. So R is not the explanation.

10. Match the monetary term with its meaning: A. Non-Performing Asset B. Bank Rate C. Open Market Operations D. Moral suasion 1. Buying and selling of government securities by the RBI 2. Informal persuasion of banks by the RBI 3. Loan whose interest or principal is overdue beyond 90 days 4. Rate at which the RBI rediscounts bills and lends long-term to banks

  1. A-3, B-4, C-1, D-2
  2. A-3, B-1, C-4, D-2
  3. A-4, B-3, C-1, D-2
  4. A-1, B-4, C-3, D-2

Answer: A) A-3, B-4, C-1, D-2

A loan is classified as an NPA when interest or principal is overdue for more than 90 days. The Bank Rate is the rate at which the RBI rediscounts bills and gives long-term funds to banks. Open Market Operations are RBI sales and purchases of government securities. Moral suasion means informal persuasion. So A-3, B-4, C-1, D-2.

11. Assertion (A): A rise in the repo rate tends to reduce demand-pull inflation. Reason (R): A higher repo rate makes bank borrowing costlier, which is passed on to customers and curbs credit-driven spending. Choose the correct option:

  1. Both A and R are true and R is the correct explanation of A
  2. Both A and R are true but R is not the correct explanation of A
  3. A is true but R is false
  4. A is false but R is true

Answer: A) Both A and R are true and R is the correct explanation of A

The Assertion is true: tightening monetary policy dampens aggregate demand and eases demand-pull price pressures. The Reason is also true and is the very mechanism: costlier funds for banks raise lending rates, discourage loans and reduce spending. Hence R correctly explains A.

18. Reserve money is also called:

  1. High-powered money
  2. Broad money
  3. Narrow money
  4. Near money

Answer: A) High-powered money

Reserve money, also called high-powered money or monetary base (M0), consists of currency in circulation, bankers' deposits with the RBI and other RBI deposits. It forms the base for the money multiplier.
