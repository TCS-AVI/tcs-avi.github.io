1. Ten suppositories each containing 200 mg of a drug are to be made in a mould calibrated for 2 g of cocoa butter. The drug has a displacement factor of 1.5. The cocoa butter required is about:

  1. 20.00 g
  2. 17.33 g
  3. 18.67 g
  4. 18.00 g

Answer: C) 18.67 g

Drug 2 g displaces 2/1.5 = 1.33 g of base; base needed = 20 - 1.33 = 18.67 g.

2. Relative bioavailability is determined by comparing the AUC of a test formulation with the AUC of:

  1. an intravenous dose
  2. a reference formulation given by the same route
  3. a placebo
  4. the same drug given only by inhalation

Answer: B) a reference formulation given by the same route

Relative bioavailability = AUC test / AUC standard, corrected for dose.

  1. is released slowly over 24 hours

7. Which property favours entry of a drug into the central nervous system across the blood-brain barrier?

  1. High polarity and ionisation
  2. Large molecular size
  3. Extensive plasma protein binding

Answer: D) High lipid solubility with low ionisation

8. Assertion (A): High-concentration syrups resist microbial growth. Reason (R): Sucrose is a disaccharide composed of glucose and fructose.

  1. Both A and R are true and R is the correct explanation of A
  2. Both A and R are true but R is not the correct explanation of A
  3. A is true but R is false
  4. A is false but R is true

Answer: B) Both A and R are true but R is not the correct explanation of A

The osmotic pressure of concentrated sucrose inhibits microbes, not the fact that it is a disaccharide.

9. Capping of tablets during compression is mainly due to:

  1. entrapped air and excessive fines in the granules
  2. excess binder causing sticking to the punch
  3. a too-soft granule with high moisture

Answer: A) entrapped air and excessive fines in the granules

Capping and lamination relate to air entrapment and poor bonding.

10. Consider the statements about suspensions: 1. Flocculated suspensions form a hard cake that cannot be redispersed. 2. Flocculated suspensions are easily redispersed on shaking. 3. Flocculated suspensions settle rapidly and leave a clear supernatant. Which of the statements are correct?

  1. 1 and 2 only
  2. 2 and 3 only

Flocculates form a loose, voluminous sediment that settles quickly and redisperses easily; hard caking is typical of deflocculated systems.

25. A weakly acidic drug is absorbed well from the stomach mainly because it is:

  2. largely unionised at gastric pH, favouring passive diffusion

34. The partition coefficient of a drug is best defined as the:

  1. ratio of drug concentration in the oil phase to that in the aqueous phase at equilibrium
  2. ratio of ionised to unionised drug at a given pH
  3. ratio of dissolution rate to diffusion rate
  4. ratio of drug absorbed to drug administered

Answer: A) ratio of drug concentration in the oil phase to that in the aqueous phase at equilibrium

Partition coefficient = C(oil)/C(water) at equilibrium and is an index of lipophilicity.

40. According to Stokes' law, the rate of sedimentation of a particle is directly proportional to the:

  1. Viscosity of the medium
  2. Cube of the particle diameter
  3. Square of the particle diameter
  4. Square root of the density difference
