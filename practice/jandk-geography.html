1. Consider the following statements about hydroelectric projects: 1. The Salal project is on the Chenab in Reasi district. 2. The Uri project is on the Jhelum in Baramulla district. 3. The Baglihar project is on the Jhelum. Which of the statements given above is/are correct?

  1. 1 and 3 only
  2. 2 and 3 only
  3. 3 only
  4. 1 and 2 only

Answer: D) 1 and 2 only

Salal stands on the Chenab in Reasi, so statement 1 is true. Uri is on the Jhelum in Baramulla, so statement 2 is true. Baglihar is on the Chenab in Ramban district, so statement 3 is false.

2. Assertion (A): Under the Indus Waters Treaty, India has unrestricted use of the waters of the Jhelum, Chenab and Indus. Reason (R): The Indus Waters Treaty of 1960 was brokered with the help of the World Bank. Select the correct answer.

  1. Both A and R are true and R is the correct explanation of A
  2. Both A and R are true but R is not the correct explanation of A
  3. A is true but R is false
  4. A is false but R is true

Answer: D) A is false but R is true

Assertion is false: the treaty gave India the eastern rivers Ravi, Beas and Sutlej, while the western rivers Indus, Jhelum and Chenab went to Pakistan, with India allowed limited uses such as run-of-river hydropower. Reason is true: the World Bank was a broker and signatory in Karachi in 1960.

3. Consider the following statements about the Udhampur-Srinagar-Baramulla Rail Link: 1. The Chenab rail bridge is located in Reasi district. 2. The Chenab rail bridge is a cable-stayed bridge. 3. USBRL stands for Udhampur-Srinagar-Baramulla Rail Link. Which of the statements given above is/are correct?

  1. 1 and 2 only
  2. 2 and 3 only
  3. 1 and 3 only
  4. 1, 2 and 3

Answer: C) 1 and 3 only

The Chenab rail bridge lies near Bakkal and Kauri in Reasi district, so statement 1 is true. It is a steel arch bridge; the cable-stayed bridge on the line is Anji Khad, so statement 2 is false. USBRL expands correctly in statement 3.

4. Consider the following statements about the fauna of Jammu and Kashmir: 1. The Hangul is found in the Dachigam National Park. 2. The black-necked crane is the State bird of Jammu and Kashmir. 3. The black-necked crane breeds in the wetlands of the Kashmir valley. Which of the statements given above is/are correct?

  1. 1 only
  2. 2 only
  3. 1 and 2 only
  4. 1 and 3 only

Answer: C) 1 and 2 only

The Hangul or Kashmir stag survives in Dachigam, so statement 1 is true. The black-necked crane is the State bird, so statement 2 is true. It breeds on high-altitude wetlands of eastern Ladakh (Changthang), not the Kashmir valley, so statement 3 is false.

5. All of the following are tributaries of the Chenab in Jammu and Kashmir EXCEPT

  1. Marusudar
  2. Neeru
  3. Lidder
  4. Tawi

Answer: C) Lidder

Marusudar (Marwah) joins the Chenab in the Kishtwar area. Neeru joins it in the Doda region. Tawi, which flows past Jammu city, joins the Chenab. The Lidder rises from the Kolahoi glacier and joins the Jhelum near Anantnag, so it is the exception.

7. Consider the following statements about road tunnels: 1. The Jawahar Tunnel at Banihal was opened in 1956. 2. The Chenani-Nashri tunnel was opened in 2017. 3. The Jawahar Tunnel passes under the Zojila pass. Which of the statements given above is/are correct?

  2. 1 and 2 only

The Jawahar Tunnel through the Pir Panjal at Banihal was opened in 1956, so statement 1 is true. The Chenani-Nashri (Patnitop) tunnel was opened in April 2017, so statement 2 is true. Zojila is a separate pass on the Srinagar-Leh road, so statement 3 is false.

9. Consider the following statements about the lakes of Jammu and Kashmir and Ladakh: 1. Hokersar near Srinagar is a Ramsar site. 2. Pangong Tso is a freshwater lake. 3. Manasbal Lake is located in Ganderbal district. Which of the statements given above is/are correct?

10. Consider the following statements about mountain passes: 1. Zojila connects the Kashmir valley with Ladakh. 2. Banihal Pass lies in the Pir Panjal range. 3. Sinthan Pass connects the Kashmir valley with Ladakh. Which of the statements given above is/are correct?

11. Consider the following statements about soils and relief of Jammu and Kashmir: 1. Karewas are lacustrine deposits of the Kashmir valley. 2. Karewas are suitable for crops such as saffron, almond and apple. 3. The Kandi belt of Jammu is a fertile alluvial plain ideal for rice. Which of the statements given above is/are correct?

  1. 2 only
  2. 1 and 2 only
  3. 1 and 3 only
  4. 2 and 3 only

Answer: B) 1 and 2 only

Karewas are old lake-bed deposits of the Kashmir valley, so statement 1 is true. Their well-drained plateaus suit saffron, almond and apple, so statement 2 is true. The Kandi belt is a sub-mountainous, erosion-prone, mostly rainfed tract, not a fertile alluvial rice plain, so statement 3 is false.

12. Assertion (A): The Hangul is found in the Dachigam National Park. Reason (R): The Hangul is a species of musk deer. Select the correct answer.

  1. Both A and R are true and R is the correct explanation of A
  2. Both A and R are true but R is not the correct explanation of A
  3. A is true but R is false
  4. A is false but R is true

Answer: C) A is true but R is false

Assertion is true: Dachigam near Srinagar is the last stronghold of the Hangul or Kashmir stag. Reason is false: the Hangul is a red deer subspecies (Cervus hanglu hanglu), whereas the musk deer is a different animal.

14. Pangong Tso in Ladakh is which type of lake?

  1. Saline (brackish) lake
  2. Freshwater lake fed by the Indus
  3. Man-made reservoir
  4. Oxbow lake of the Jhelum

Answer: A) Saline (brackish) lake

Pangong Tso is a long endorheic lake with no outlet, so salts accumulate and the water is brackish despite being fed by glacial meltwater. About one third lies in India and the rest in Tibet, China. Memory tip: no outlet means salty.

16. The Zojila Pass connects the Kashmir Valley with which region?

  1. Jammu plains
  2. Himachal Pradesh
  3. Ladakh
  4. Punjab

18. The lacustrine plateau deposits of the Kashmir Valley are known as:

  1. Bhabar
  2. Terai
  3. Karewas
  4. Khadar

Answer: C) Karewas

Karewas are thick lacustrine (lake) deposits found on plateaus of the Kashmir Valley, dating to the Pleistocene. They are fertile for saffron, almonds and apples. Bhabar, Terai and Khadar are features of the northern plains.
