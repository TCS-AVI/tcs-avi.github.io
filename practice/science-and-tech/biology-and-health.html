1. Which one of the following is NOT correct?

  1. Chlorophyll contains magnesium
  2. Haemoglobin contains iron
  3. Vitamin B12 contains cobalt
  4. Thyroxine contains calcium

Answer: D) Thyroxine contains calcium

Chlorophyll has magnesium at the centre of its porphyrin ring. Haemoglobin has iron in haem. Vitamin B12 (cyanocobalamin) contains cobalt. Thyroxine is an iodine-containing hormone of the thyroid gland, not calcium-containing, so the last statement is incorrect.

2. Consider the following statements: 1. Mature red blood cells of mammals lack a nucleus. 2. Insulin is secreted by the alpha cells of the islets of Langerhans. 3. Haemoglobin contains iron. Which of the statements given above is/are correct?

  1. 1 and 2 only
  2. 2 and 3 only
  3. 1 and 3 only
  4. 1, 2 and 3

Answer: C) 1 and 3 only

Statement 1 is true: this leaves more room for haemoglobin. Statement 2 is false: beta cells secrete insulin; alpha cells secrete glucagon. Statement 3 is true: iron in haem binds oxygen. So the answer is 1 and 3 only.

3. Consider the following statements: 1. Vitamin K is required for the synthesis of certain blood clotting factors. 2. Pellagra is caused by deficiency of niacin (vitamin B3). 3. Scurvy is caused by deficiency of vitamin B12. Which of the statements given above is/are correct?

  1. 1 and 2 only
  2. 3 only
  3. 1 and 3 only
  4. 2 and 3 only

Answer: A) 1 and 2 only

Statement 1 is true: its deficiency delays clotting. Statement 2 is true: it shows dermatitis, diarrhoea and dementia. Statement 3 is false: scurvy is due to vitamin C deficiency. So the answer is 1 and 2 only.

4. Where are red blood cells produced in adults?

  1. Liver
  2. Bone marrow
  3. Spleen
  4. Kidney

Answer: B) Bone marrow

In adults, red blood cells are made in the red bone marrow of bones such as the sternum and pelvis. In the foetus, the liver and spleen make them. Their life span is about 120 days.

5. Malaria is caused by which type of organism?

  1. Virus
  2. Bacterium
  3. Protozoan (Plasmodium)
  4. Fungus

Answer: C) Protozoan (Plasmodium)

Malaria is caused by the protozoan parasite Plasmodium, spread by the bite of the female Anopheles mosquito. Main species include P. vivax and P. falciparum, the deadliest.

6. Which enzyme in saliva begins digestion of starch?

  1. Pepsin
  2. Lipase
  3. Salivary amylase
  4. Trypsin

Answer: C) Salivary amylase

Salivary amylase (ptyalin) in saliva starts the breakdown of starch into maltose in the mouth. Pepsin acts on proteins in the stomach. Memory tip: Amylase = starch.

8. Which gland is called the master gland?

  1. Thyroid
  2. Pituitary
  3. Pancreas
  4. Adrenal

Answer: B) Pituitary

The pituitary, a pea-sized gland at the base of the brain, is the master gland because its hormones control the thyroid, adrenals and gonads. It is itself regulated by the hypothalamus.

9. Which of these is an insectivorous plant?

  1. Pitcher plant
  2. Mango
  3. Hibiscus
  4. Bamboo

Answer: A) Pitcher plant

Pitcher plant is insectivorous: its pitcher-shaped leaf traps insects and digests them to get nitrogen from poor soil. Other examples are sundew, Venus flytrap and bladderwort.

10. Which of these diseases is caused by a bacterium?

  1. Polio
  2. Mumps
  3. Cholera
  4. Dengue

Answer: C) Cholera

Cholera is a bacterial disease caused by Vibrio cholerae, spread through contaminated water and treated with oral rehydration solution. Polio, mumps and dengue are all viral.

11. Dengue is spread by which mosquito?

  1. Anopheles
  2. Culex
  3. Mansonia
  4. Aedes

Answer: D) Aedes

Dengue is a viral disease transmitted by the Aedes mosquito, mainly Aedes aegypti, which bites in the daytime and breeds in clean stagnant water. Memory tip: Aedes = Dengue; Anopheles = Malaria.

12. The loss of water vapour from the aerial parts of plants is called?

  1. Transpiration
  2. Respiration
  3. Germination
  4. Guttation only

Answer: A) Transpiration

Transpiration is the loss of water vapour from aerial parts of plants, mostly through stomata of leaves. It pulls water upwards and cools the plant. Guttation is loss of liquid water through hydathodes.

13. Photosynthesis takes place in which cell organelle?

  1. Mitochondria
  2. Ribosome
  3. Nucleus
  4. Chloroplast

Answer: D) Chloroplast

Photosynthesis occurs in chloroplasts, which contain chlorophyll in stacks called grana. The overall reaction makes glucose and oxygen from carbon dioxide and water using light. Mitochondria do respiration.

14. Tuberculosis is caused by which type of organism?

  1. Bacterium
  2. Virus
  3. Protozoan
  4. Fungus

Answer: A) Bacterium

Tuberculosis is a bacterial disease of the lungs caused by Mycobacterium tuberculosis, discovered by Robert Koch in 1882. It spreads by air droplets, and the BCG vaccine protects against it.

15. Which cell organelle controls the activities of the cell?

  1. Mitochondria
  2. Vacuole
  3. Nucleus
  4. Ribosome

Answer: C) Nucleus

The nucleus holds the chromosomes and DNA and so directs growth, metabolism and reproduction; it is called the control centre of the cell. It was discovered by Robert Brown in 1831.

17. Deficiency of which vitamin causes scurvy?

  1. Vitamin A
  2. Vitamin D
  3. Vitamin K
  4. Vitamin C

Answer: D) Vitamin C

Scurvy results from vitamin C (ascorbic acid) deficiency, causing bleeding gums, poor wound healing and joint pain. Citrus fruits, amla and guava are rich sources. Memory tip: C for scurvy, Citrus cures.

18. Which vitamin is essential for blood clotting?

  1. Vitamin A
  2. Vitamin C
  3. Vitamin E
  4. Vitamin K

Answer: D) Vitamin K

Vitamin K is needed by the liver to make clotting factors such as prothrombin, so its lack delays blood clotting. K comes from the German word Koagulation. Green leafy vegetables are a good source.

19. What is the natural pacemaker of the human heart?

  1. Atrioventricular node
  2. Bundle of His
  3. Purkinje fibres
  4. Sinoatrial (SA) node

Answer: D) Sinoatrial (SA) node

The sinoatrial (SA) node in the right atrium generates the electrical impulses that start each heartbeat, so it is the natural pacemaker. An artificial pacemaker replaces it when it fails.

24. Which gland secretes insulin?

  1. Liver
  2. Thyroid
  3. Adrenal
  4. Pancreas

Answer: D) Pancreas

Insulin is produced by the beta cells of the islets of Langerhans in the pancreas and lowers blood glucose. Its deficiency causes diabetes mellitus. Banting and Best discovered insulin in 1921.

27. Which blood group is called the universal donor?

  1. AB positive
  2. O negative
  3. A positive
  4. B negative

Answer: B) O negative

O negative is the universal donor because its red cells lack A, B and Rh antigens, so recipients' antibodies do not attack them. In emergencies O negative blood is given.

28. Which blood group is called the universal recipient?

  1. O negative
  2. A negative
  3. AB positive
  4. B positive

Answer: C) AB positive

AB positive is the universal recipient because it has A, B and Rh antigens and no anti-A or anti-B antibodies in plasma, so it can receive red cells of any group.

  1. Chlorophyll
  2. Carotene
  3. Xanthophyll
  4. Anthocyanin

Answer: A) Chlorophyll

Chlorophyll is the main photosynthetic pigment and reflects green light, so leaves look green. It absorbs mainly red and blue light. Memory tip: chloro = green, phyll = leaf; it contains magnesium.

  1. Lungs
  2. Kidneys
  3. Heart
  4. Liver

Answer: D) Liver

Hepatitis means inflammation of the liver, commonly caused by hepatitis A to E viruses. A and E spread through contaminated food and water; B and C through blood. Vaccines exist for A and B.

31. How many chromosomes does a normal human body cell have?

  1. 23
  2. 46
  3. 48
  4. 44

Answer: B) 46

A normal human body cell has 46 chromosomes arranged as 23 pairs, 22 autosome pairs and one pair of sex chromosomes. Gametes carry 23. Down syndrome has 47 due to an extra chromosome 21.

37. How many chambers does the human heart have?

  1. Four
  2. Two
  3. Three
  4. Five

Answer: A) Four

The human heart has four chambers: two upper atria and two lower ventricles. This fully separates oxygenated and deoxygenated blood. Fish have two chambers; amphibians have three.

  1. Stomach
  2. Small intestine
  3. Large intestine
  4. Oesophagus

Answer: B) Small intestine

Most absorption of digested food happens in the small intestine, whose inner wall has finger-like villi that increase surface area. The large intestine mainly absorbs water. Memory tip: Villi = absorption.
