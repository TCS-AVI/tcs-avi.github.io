1. Salbutamol resists metabolism by COMT because

  1. it has an N-tert-butyl group
  2. it lacks any aromatic ring
  3. its 3-hydroxyl group of catechol is replaced by a hydroxymethyl group
  4. it is a quaternary ammonium compound

Answer: C) its 3-hydroxyl group of catechol is replaced by a hydroxymethyl group

A saligenin ring removes the catechol, so COMT cannot methylate it.

2. The van Deemter equation in chromatography relates

  1. absorbance to concentration
  2. Rf value to solvent polarity
  3. pressure to column temperature
  4. plate height (HETP) to the linear velocity of the mobile phase

Answer: D) plate height (HETP) to the linear velocity of the mobile phase

H = A + B/u + Cu, describing band broadening from eddy diffusion, longitudinal diffusion and mass transfer.

8. In an iodometric titration with sodium thiosulphate, starch solution should be added

  1. near the end point, when the iodine colour has faded to pale yellow
  2. at the very beginning of the titration
  3. after the end point has been passed
  4. only if the solution is alkaline

Answer: A) near the end point, when the iodine colour has faded to pale yellow

Added early, starch adsorbs much iodine as a stable blue complex that reacts slowly, causing a sluggish end point.

11. Assertion (A): SDS-PAGE separates proteins mainly on the basis of molecular size. Reason (R): Proteins show different colours when stained with Coomassie brilliant blue. Choose the correct option.

  1. Both A and R are true and R is the correct explanation of A
  2. Both A and R are true but R is not the correct explanation of A
  3. A is true but R is false
  4. A is false but R is true

Answer: B) Both A and R are true but R is not the correct explanation of A

SDS gives proteins a uniform charge-to-mass ratio so size determines migration; staining colour is unrelated to separation.

12. Boric acid is titrated with sodium hydroxide only after adding glycerol or mannitol because the polyol

  1. acts as the indicator
  2. oxidises boric acid
  3. prevents absorption of carbon dioxide
  4. forms a stronger acid complex that gives a sharp end point

Answer: D) forms a stronger acid complex that gives a sharp end point

Boric acid is too weak (pKa about 9) for a sharp end point; polyhydroxy compounds form a stronger monobasic complex acid.

13. When an amine hydrochloride (for example ephedrine hydrochloride) is titrated with perchloric acid in glacial acetic acid, which reagent is added to remove the interfering halide?

  1. Sodium acetate
  2. Acetic anhydride
  3. Potassium chloride
  4. Mercuric acetate

Answer: D) Mercuric acetate

Mercuric acetate converts chloride to undissociated mercuric chloride and liberates an equivalent amount of acetate, a titratable base.

35. An optically active solution at 0.1 g/mL gives an observed rotation of +5.0° in a 2 dm tube. Its specific rotation is

  1. +50°
  2. +25°
  3. +2.5°
  4. +100°

[alpha] = alpha/(l x c) = 5.0/(2 x 0.1) = +25.

40. Consider the statements on stereochemistry: 1. The R/S designation always corresponds to the d/l (+/-) direction of rotation. 2. Enantiomers rotate plane-polarised light by equal amounts in opposite directions. 3. Ephedrine and pseudoephedrine are diastereomers. Which are correct?

45. Calcium carbonate, which is insoluble in water, is assayed by adding a known excess of standard hydrochloric acid and titrating the unreacted acid with sodium hydroxide. This type of titration is called

  1. a back (residual) titration
  2. a direct titration
  3. a displacement titration
  4. an iodometric titration

Answer: A) a back (residual) titration

Back titration is used for insoluble or slowly reacting substances: excess reagent is added and the leftover is titrated.
